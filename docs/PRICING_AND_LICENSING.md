# What costs what

Nothing, yet: there is nowhere to buy a licence and the program asks for none. This
page is what will be true when there is, written down in advance so that it is a
decision rather than something improvised at a checkout.

## The shape of it

**Free, and staying free.** Opening any Android or iPhone backup, reading it,
searching it, the verification report, and `amberkeep canary`. Somebody whose phone
has died can get their history onto a screen and read it without paying anybody. That
is not a trial and it does not expire.

The engine and the command-line tool are AGPL-3.0. Anybody can build them from source
and use everything, forever, including the parts a licence unlocks in the application.
That is not a loophole; it is the licence working as intended, and the reason to buy
is the application and the support rather than the capability.

**Archive licence — 29 EUR at launch, 39 EUR after.** Writing the archive out: web
pages, plain text, structured data, and printing. Two computers. Twelve months of
updates, and the version you were entitled to keeps working forever — a licence that
stops opening your own history when it lapses would be a hostage, not a licence.

**Migration — 29 EUR more, or 59 EUR for both.** Moving an Android history onto an
iPhone: the checks, the plan, the written backup, the guide. Unlimited attempts for
one pair of phones, because getting it wrong the first time is common and charging
somebody twice for that would be indecent.

Never below 20 EUR. A tool that reads somebody's entire correspondence and writes into
a copy of their phone's backup is not a thing to sell at a price that says it was
cheap to make.

## How a licence works

**It is a file, and it works with no network.** This program makes no network calls at
all, and a licence is not going to be the exception that phones home. A key is a
signed statement — "this licence covers the archive and the migration, issued on this
day" — verified against a public key built into the program. Nothing is checked with
a server, at purchase or ever, because there is no server.

**No account.** No email to register, no password to lose, no profile. The key arrives
after payment and is kept wherever the buyer keeps such things.

**Two computers** is a statement of what is fair rather than something the program
enforces, since an offline licence cannot count machines it has never heard of. It is
written down because most people want to do the right thing and need to know what that
is.

**Nothing is taken away.** A lapsed licence does not close an archive, delete an
export or lock a screen. It stops new versions from unlocking new things, and that is
the whole of it.

## Buying it

Through a **merchant of record** — Paddle — which means they are the seller of record
and handle VAT in every country somebody buys from. It costs 5% plus 50 cents a sale
and removes an entire class of problem that is otherwise a solo developer's evenings
for the rest of their life.

## Refunds

**Thirty days, no questions, no argument.** Ask and it is refunded.

This is written in plain words on purpose. Every competitor in this market is hated
for the opposite: subscriptions that renew silently, a "discount" that never ends, and
a refund you have to fight for. A tool asking to be trusted with somebody's whole
message history cannot be in that company.

The free tier is what makes it honest. Anybody can open their own archive and see
their own conversations on screen before any money is discussed, so nobody is ever
buying a promise that it will work on their data.

## What is not sold

- **Anybody's data.** There is nothing to sell: nothing leaves the machine.
- **Subscriptions.** One payment, for what exists when it is made.
- **Attention.** No analytics, no crash reports without being asked, no newsletter you
  did not choose.
