# The mark

A placeholder, and deliberately labelled as one. It is on brief, it is 1.2 KB of SVG
with no dependencies, and it can be replaced by overwriting the five files here and
rebuilding. It should not be trademarked, printed, or treated as settled.

| File | For |
|---|---|
| `mark.svg` | 48 px and up: the full drawing, legs and antennae |
| `mark-small.svg` | 24–48 px: antennae dropped, legs thickened. This is the favicon |
| `mark-micro.svg` | 16–20 px: three masses, no legs at all |
| `mark-mono.svg` | One colour, dark on pale |
| `mark-mono-reversed.svg` | One colour, pale on dark |

Separate drawings rather than one artwork scaled, because a 0.2-pixel stroke is not a
leg. That is the whole reason there is more than one file.

## How it is built

The ant is cut **out** of the amber rather than drawn on top of it. That is why there
is no light-theme and dark-theme version: on a pale ground the ant comes back pale, on
a dark ground it comes back dark, and the same file is correct in both places. It also
means the mark survives being flattened to one colour, which a painted-on inclusion
would not.

Flat fill throughout. No gradient, no highlight.

## The brief, for whoever draws the real one

Binding, from the naming rules in the product plan:

- **Never green**, and never a speech bubble. Both are Meta's, and Meta enforces.
- Never "WhatsApp" or a phonetic variant, in the mark or anywhere near it.
- It has to hold at **16 px** and survive **one colour**.

And three things already tried and rejected, so nobody spends the time twice:

1. **A glossy oval.** Radial gradient, specular highlight, hairline legs at 1.3% of
   the canvas. Skeuomorphic, a decade out of date, and the legs vanish below 32 px.
2. **A teardrop container.** Collides with the water-drop icon and the map pin, which
   are two of the most-used glyphs in software. No silhouette of its own.
3. **No container at all.** Fixes the generic silhouette and destroys the idea: without
   the resin the ant reads as a spider, and nothing about it says preservation.

What is still unsolved is the container. The three attempts produced an oval, a
teardrop and a circle. The ant itself is finished and can be kept.
